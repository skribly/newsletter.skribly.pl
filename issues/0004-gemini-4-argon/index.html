Gemini 4 Argon arrives

Google's new frontier model for developers, scientists and the Gemini app

On 13 October 2026 Google released Gemini 4 Argon, which it calls its latest frontier model. Developers and businesses can use it worldwide through the Gemini API in Google AI Studio; it is also in Google Antigravity, the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search for Google AI subscribers. Through the API it takes a combined limit of one million tokens for input and output. Google reports that in its own use agentic code submissions in Antigravity rose by more than 35% and rollbacks fell by nearly a third, and that prompt-injection attacks on the Gray Swan benchmark succeeded in only 0.7% of cases. A separate post shows Argon reasoning about the physical world, with 87% on the ERQA benchmark, and steering a Spot robot.

Sources: Google: Gemini 4 Argon, built to solve your hardest problems · Google for Developers: Gemini 4 Argon for Robotics

In brief

Meta publishes the weights of Muse Spark Open

On 15 October 2026 Meta released Muse Spark Open, the open-weight version of its Muse Spark family. It is a natively multimodal model with 816 billion parameters, of which 42 billion are active, and a context of one million tokens. Meta added extra safety training before release and highlights coding and long agentic tasks. The weights come under the Muse Model License with limited commercial terms and are on Hugging Face, among other places.

Source: Meta AI: Muse Spark Open

Mistral's first models for forecasting time series

Mistral introduced Timestral on 15 October 2026, its first family of foundation models for time series. They forecast many related series at once and give a range of likely values instead of one number, also on data they have never seen. There are four sizes, from 5 to 209 million parameters, and they need no GPU to run. The licence is a modified MIT licence, and the models are on Hugging Face and GitHub.

Source: Mistral AI: Introducing Timestral

Anthropic makes copying Claude much harder

On 12 October 2026 Anthropic described how it fights distillation, the covert, large-scale harvesting of a model's answers to train another model. Its defences include a classifier with false positives under 0.1%, stricter limits for repeat offenders and tens of thousands of banned accounts. Anthropic estimates that the data distillers collect fell by about 90 to 99%, but expects a game of cat and mouse.

Source: Anthropic: Strengthening our defenses against distillation attacks

Outside evaluators get an insider's view of Anthropic

On 15 October 2026 Anthropic said it has given a team from METR, a nonprofit that evaluates AI models, "employee-like access": six people with company hardware, office space and internal tools. METR will publish its risk assessments, and Anthropic cannot veto publication, only ask to redact defined categories such as confidential material. SecureBio is reviewing the chemical and biological chapter of a risk report, and Anthropic is building three tiers of access for evaluators.

Source: Anthropic: An update on independent evaluators

Claude Code Review leaves research preview

Since 15 October 2026 Code Review in Claude Code is out of research preview for Team and Enterprise organisations. Once an admin enables it and installs the GitHub App, it reviews new pull requests automatically before a person does. Admins choose Quick, Standard or Deep per repository. Anthropic says most reviews now take about 6 to 10 minutes instead of about 20.

Source: Claude: Code Review is now generally available

A Gemma model on the phone spots scams in Singapore

On 14 October 2026 Google DeepMind described a Gemma model fine-tuned with the Singapore government to recognise scam conversations. It runs on the phone itself, so neither Google nor the government reads people's conversations: a light classifier screens messages first and passes suspicious ones to the model. GovTech Singapore has opened a beta of the Anti-Scam Pango app for Android users in Singapore.

Source: Google DeepMind: on-device AI scam detection with the Singapore Government

One thing to try

Lock the actions in your GitHub workflows

Since 14 October 2026 GitHub Actions can read a lockfile, .github/workflows/actions.lock, that records the exact commit of every action a workflow uses, so a moved tag or branch cannot change what runs. Install the gh actions-lock extension for GitHub CLI from the github/gh-actions-lock repository, generate the lockfile, review it and commit it. It is in public preview, also on Free plans. A lock pins versions but does not tell you whether an action is safe, so review it before you lock it.

Source: GitHub Changelog: GitHub Actions workflow lockfiles in public preview
